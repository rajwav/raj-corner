## 01 AN ORDINARY TUESDAY

```text
It was Tuesday, 8 September 2026.

We had a packed second half of classes from
2 PM to 5 PM, but the CRs had cancelled the
3–5 PM classes...
```

## 02 — THE COLLISION

A scooty was coming toward me from the opposite side of the road, with an ambulance just behind it. I was cycling slowly near the edge of the road, completely unaware of what was about to happen. As the scooty came closer, the rider suddenly turned the handle toward my side and accelerated. There was barely any time to react. **Within a few seconds, the scooty hit me.** I felt my phone fly out of my hand as I lost my balance. **And then I was falling.**

## 03 — THE FALL

As I lost my balance, I started falling forward. And then something strange happened. **Everything seemed to slow down.** For a few moments, I could see everything almost frame by frame. I could see myself falling and a rock or stone directly ahead of where my head was going. I was scared. My biggest fear at that moment wasn't even the cycle or the pain—it was **my head hitting that rock.** I watched myself falling toward it, almost as if I had enough time to understand exactly what was happening but not enough time to stop it. Fortunately, my head didn't take the impact I had feared. I hit the ground. I immediately felt pain in my **left leg and left hand**. And then, everything went strangely quiet.

## 04— THE SILENCE

After I hit the ground, I froze. For roughly **10–15 seconds**, I couldn't properly see or hear what was happening around me. I couldn't respond to anyone, although I could feel that something was happening around me. My mind was filled with mixed reactions—I could feel pain in my **left leg and left hand**, but at the same time I felt a huge sense of relief that my head hadn't taken the impact I was afraid of. I also remember saying, **“Bro, dekhke chalao na, yaha kaha aa gaya,”** although I still don't know whether I said it during the fall, immediately before it, or afterward.

## 05 — THE CROWD

Slowly, the silence started disappearing and I began hearing many voices around me. People were running toward me and asking, **“Raj, pani piyoge?”** Others were scolding the scooty rider, saying things like, **“Bhaiya, dekhke chalao na, injury ho gaya usse.”** At first I couldn't properly respond. I could sense everything, but it took some time for me to understand what everyone was saying. After around a minute or two, things gradually started becoming clear again.

## 06 — THE AMBULANCE

The ambulance was nearby, and my first clear response was, **“Guys, thoda side ho jao, sab ambulance ko jaane do.”** The ambulance person had already come outside to check on me. He told me, **“Babu, uska tension mat lo, gadi mein koi hai nahi. Tum bolo toh tumhe hospital le jau.”** I went quiet again for another 15–20 seconds. Then I started thinking about the people involved in the accident and asked, **“Mujhe jo gadi lagaya wo kaha hai?”** and **“Un dono ko kuch hua nahi na?”** There was a girl sitting behind the rider, so I was worried about both of them. Fortunately, I found out that they were both okay.

## 07 — TWO STRANGERS

Two brothers who happened to be there were **VIMSAR students**. I didn't know them before that day, but they immediately stepped in to help me. They helped take me to VIMSAR and managed much of the hospital process around me. They arranged the stretcher and helped with the casualty, radiology, orthopaedics, injections and other formalities. Everything moved incredibly quickly, while I was mostly lying on the stretcher and sleeping. They were complete strangers, but they simply helped when I needed it.

## 08 — THE STRETCHER

One memory I especially want to preserve is the video I took while I was being taken through the hospital on the stretcher. **Abhisekh and Nihar can be seen helping with the stretcher** in the video. I was mostly just lying there and sleeping while everything around me was being handled by other people. Looking back, that video captures something important about that day—not just that I was injured, but that people were actually there helping me when I couldn't manage everything myself.

## 09 — VIMSAR

At VIMSAR, everything happened very quickly. I went through **casualty, radiology, orthopaedics and the injection room**, with the two VIMSAR students helping manage the process. I didn't immediately get the physical X-ray films because they would take some additional time, but they managed to get immediate photographs of the X-rays and sent them to me on WhatsApp. They also helped get the medicines. Eventually, I thanked them deeply before they left, and I still have their number because of that day.

## 10 — THE X-RAYS

The X-rays became one of the physical records of that day. I didn't have the original films with me immediately, but the photographs of them were sent to me through WhatsApp. They documented the examination of my **left leg and other areas checked after the accident**. Looking at those images now feels strange because they are a permanent record of something that happened within just a few seconds on an otherwise completely ordinary Tuesday.

## 11 — THE PEOPLE WHO TOOK CARE OF EVERYTHING

While I was at the hospital, my friends made sure that everything I had left behind was safe. **Abhisekh, Asutosh, Nihar and Niranjan** took care of my cycle, bag and belongings while I was away. **Preeti, Swastika, Adhyasha and Sijal** also supported me during and after the accident. I didn't have to worry about what was happening to my things because other people had already taken care of it.

## 12 — ABHISEKH & NIHAR

After I reached the hospital, **Abhisekh and Nihar** came to me and stayed with me throughout the process. After the two VIMSAR students had arranged the medicines and handed everything over to them, they continued taking care of me. They collected my medicines, booked an auto and helped me return to the hostel. They stayed with me until I reached my room safely.

## 13 — BACK AT THE HOSTEL

After I reached the hostel, **Satyam, Samvab, Amit, Janmejay, Swati, Shakti, Ajay, Priyanshu, Abhijeet, Pradipta and Snehanshu** checked on me. Many other hostel guys also came to see how I was doing. After a long day at the hospital, it felt good knowing that people were still checking on me after I returned.

## 14 — JERRY

**Jerry, my roommate, helped me with everything in the room** after I came back. Being injured made even ordinary things more difficult, and he was there to help whenever I needed it. My other roommate didn't even ask how I was. I don't want this memory to become about blaming anyone, but I do want to remember who actually showed up when I needed help. Jerry did.

## 15 — THE LEFT HAND STORY

The injuries from this accident were mainly to my **left leg and left hand**. But my left hand already had a history with cycling. Around three months earlier, I had suffered a **minor dislocation of my left hand from another cycling accident**, which also happened because of this cycle. So the injury to my left hand this time wasn't an entirely new story—it came after an earlier injury that had happened only a few months before.

## 16 — THE REPLACEMENT CYCLE

After the previous accident, the cycle was replaced with a **new model under the replacement policy**. The replacement arrived in **Bhubaneswar**, while I was in **Sambalpur**, so I had to wait because the package was large and needed to be transported manually over the long distance. After around three months, when my brother finally had proper time to bring it, I received the new cycle and unboxed it.

After some time, my parents agreed to let me use the cycle again. Before this accident, I had also repaired the cycle myself. I removed the **front wheel**, carried it around **4 km to the mart**, got it brought back into proper shape and had **new spokes/rods fitted**. I then manually reinstalled the wheel onto the cycle myself.

And then, unbelievably, **just 7–8 days after unboxing the replacement cycle, I had another accident.** 😭

## 17 — MY PARENTS

After my previous accident, my parents were understandably worried, but they eventually agreed to let me use the cycle again. They even supported me in getting it ready, ordering a **mudguard, lights, horn and other accessories** for it. After waiting around three months for the replacement cycle to reach me, repairing the front wheel and getting everything ready, I finally started using it again.

Then, just **7–8 days after getting the replacement cycle**, this accident happened. Seeing me injured again made my parents much more scared, and this time they became strict and started telling me not to use the cycle anymore.

I understood that their strictness wasn't really anger. They had already seen me get hurt once, then supported me in getting back on the cycle, only to see me injured again within days. **When they said “cycle mat chalao,” what they really meant was, “We don't want anything to happen to you again.”** ❤️

## 18 — WHAT PEOPLE SAW VS. WHAT I FELT

Some people saw me after the accident and treated it as if it were nothing. Some said I was **acting**, exaggerating my injury, trying to get attention, or even **“marketing” my injury**. I understand why someone who only saw me afterward might think that way—I could talk normally, smile and joke around, and the injuries didn't necessarily look as serious as they felt. But they didn't see the collision, the fear during the fall, the strange silence afterward, the confusion, the pain or how difficult it was for me to walk. I wasn't trying to prove that I was injured. If anything, I was doing the opposite: **something significant had happened, but I was behaving as if everything was normal.**

## 19 — WHAT I WANT TO REMEMBER

An ordinary Tuesday afternoon became a day I never expected to remember this clearly. I remember the slow-motion feeling during the fall, being scared that my head would hit the rock, the silence afterward, slowly hearing the crowd again, and worrying about the people on the scooty before properly thinking about myself. But I also remember the two strangers who helped me, Abhisekh and Nihar helping me through the hospital and back to the hostel, my friends taking care of my belongings, Jerry helping me with everything in the room, everyone who checked on me, and my parents being scared for me. **The accident is what happened to me. The people who showed up are what I want to remember.** ❤️

# WHAT I LEARNED

* **Life can change before you even realise it.** I was just returning from class, slowly cycling and looking for a song, and within a few seconds I was on the road, surrounded by people and being taken to a hospital. It made me realise that an ordinary moment should never be treated as something guaranteed.
* **The people who show up matter more than the people who judge.** I may remember the accident, but I will remember the strangers who helped me, the friends who protected my things, Abhisekh and Nihar staying with me, Jerry helping me in my room, and my parents worrying about me. **A difficult moment reveals who is actually there.** At the same time, it also made me rethink some relationships. There were people I had considered very close to me, people I genuinely believed cared deeply about me, including SK and Banamudra, whose response to what happened felt much less involved than I had expected. I don't want to judge them or turn this memory into resentment, but it did teach me something about relationships: **sometimes the people we think are closest to us aren't necessarily the people who show up when we need them, and sometimes care comes from places we never expected.**
* **Sometimes love looks like fear and strictness.** My parents had trusted me again, helped me prepare the new cycle and even ordered things for it. When they became strict after this accident, I understood that behind their words was not anger—it was the fear of losing or seeing their son hurt.
* **I was luckier than I realised.** My head came dangerously close to a rock, yet I escaped a potentially much worse injury. Sometimes you only understand how close something came to being much worse after the moment has passed.
* **I don't want to remember this day only as the day I got hurt.** I want to remember it as the day I discovered how quickly life can change, how fragile an ordinary moment can be, and how many people can stand beside you when everything suddenly goes wrong.
