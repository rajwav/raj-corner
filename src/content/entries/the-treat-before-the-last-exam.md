October 15 was the day after our second-last midsem exam.

Technically, we still had one exam left on the schedule—Professional Ethics. But almost nobody was taking that one too seriously, so for all practical purposes, it felt like the midsems were already behind us.

After the exam wrapped up, the four of us—Ansika, Susmita, Banamudra, and I—met up.

Our first stop was the DSW office. We had some work regarding an attendance sheet, but the teachers weren't showing up. We waited around for a while, realized nothing was going to happen, and eventually gave up and walked back out without getting the work done.

And then came the treat.

Banamudra decided to treat us.

Somewhere along the way, Banamudra mentioned that she hadn't slept or eaten properly for the last two days. That prompted Susmita to ask what the secret was to hiding dark circles when you're neither eating nor sleeping.

I chimed in with something along the lines of keeping your face clean.

To this day, I have no idea why I said that. I suppose I didn't have any better answer ready. I always make an effort to keep my face clean, but that hardly qualifies me as an expert on hiding dark circles.

Naturally, the conversation drifted back to our exams. Banamudra and I started dissecting how terribly some of the earlier papers had gone, and how, against all odds, we had somehow managed to do better in the last one or two despite the questions being absurdly lengthy. We wondered aloud how endsems would treat us. It was that classic post-exam rhythm—complaining about the question papers while quietly feeling surprised that we were surviving them at all.

Then we reached the pani puri stall.

I decided to try whatever Banamudra was having. We all went for the plate-style pani puri—first the regular teekha version, followed by an extra-teekha round. Two plates in total.

Everyone else polished theirs off without trouble, but I ran into a wall: my jaw was hurting. I couldn't finish what was on my plate. In the end, finishing my pani puri turned into a collective team effort, with everyone stepping in to help clear it.

From there, Banamudra invited us for badam shake.

I was definitely tempted, even though my initial instinct was to say no. But she said she was heading over anyway, so I changed my mind and tagged along. The others were asking about some green ice cream, while Banamudra and I opted for badam shake with ice cream. I can't recall what the drink was officially called or every ingredient that went into it, but it tasted so good.

Unfortunately, my jaw had other plans.

It didn't hurt much when I was simply talking, but the moment I had to open my mouth to eat or drink, the pain flared right up. I had to swallow the shake in this awkward, strained way, and Ansika immediately caught on. She pointed out that it looked like I was drinking forcefully. I was genuinely trying to enjoy the shake, but the discomfort made it hard work.

Even with the pain, that drink was good enough that I found myself thinking I'd love to bring everyone back here someday and treat them to the exact same badam shake.

Afterwards, they wanted dosa. With my jaw in the state it was in, eating anything solid was out of the question, so I decided to head back.

I came away with two photographs from that afternoon.

Neither of them is a polished picture. In the group photo, none of the three girls was actually prepared for the camera, each looking off in her own direction. And the other is just a half-eaten ice cream where nobody seemed particularly interested in finishing the biscuit.

Yet that is precisely why I wanted to keep them. They capture the loose, slightly disorganized, and completely real mood of that afternoon. And whenever I think back to it, I still hope to give everyone that same badam shake one day—assuming, of course, that they're willing to come along.
