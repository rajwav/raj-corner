### The Most Unsettling Idea in Physics

Before Einstein, time was treated as a universal clock ticking uniformly everywhere in the cosmos. Special and General Relativity shattered that illusion completely: time runs slower near massive gravitational wells and for observers moving at extreme velocities. 

Clocks on GPS satellites actually tick faster than clocks on the surface of the Earth by microseconds each day. Fall past the event horizon of a supermassive black hole, and the entire future of the outside universe flashes past before you reach the singularity.

The concept that time travel into the future is not science fiction—it is an observed, mathematically verified law of physics—makes everyday reality feel like a paper-thin surface.
