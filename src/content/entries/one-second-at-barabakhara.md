# CHAPTER 01 — THE PLAN

### *“Holiday hai, kuch karte hain.”*

The plan started yesterday. There wasn't any big reason—we just had a long stretch of holidays ahead of us and Priyanshu and I basically decided, **“Holiday hai, kuch karte hain.”** Barabakhara Waterfall became the plan.

Abhijeet joined too, which meant we needed another scooty. We already had Priyanshu's scooty at Bargarh, so I called the number Priyanshu sent me and arranged another one. We asked Abhijeet to come to **Kirba Chowk**, and once he gave us the scooty, there was no reason to go back to the hostel. We could start directly from there.

### ☀️ A Normal Morning

The next morning, I woke up around **8:00 AM** and went to the fourth floor to brush in the sunlight. I talked with Mama and Bhai, then met Priyanshu and Abhijeet.\
 Nothing felt particularly special. **It was just a normal morning.**\
 I was riding for most of the journey from the beginning. Around **8:30 AM**, we started.

> *Three friends. Two scooties. One waterfall.*

At that point, none of us knew what kind of day we had just signed up for.

# CHAPTER 02 — THE SCOOTY HAD OTHER PLANS

### *It started dying before we even reached the waterfall.*

We had barely gone around **25 km, near Attabira**, when the second scooty started giving us trouble. It simply refused to start properly. We stopped, talked to some locals, figured out what we could, and somehow managed to get it going again.

About **10 km later**, it happened again.

At this point, the trip had already acquired its first unofficial rule:

> **Don't stop the scooty.**

Of course, we completely failed to follow our own rule. 😭

### 🧢 The Cap Incident

After another restart, Abhijeet was riding. We were going along the road when I saw some random guys and casually waved at them.

**“Hello brother!”**

Then the wind decided to participate. My cap flew straight off my head and landed near those same guys.

The scooty stopped again. So we had to turn back. They asked what I had said to them, I talked with them for a bit, and then ran back towards the scooty.\
 At this point, we hadn't even reached Barabakhara and the journey was already producing stories.

### 🍈 A Five-Minute Pit Stop

Somehow we made it to Priyanshu's house. He told us to wait about five minutes, then came back with **guavas**.

We ate them, waited another 5–10 minutes, and tried the scooty again. Then somehow, **I got it started.** We decided we simply wouldn't stop it anymore.

That lasted until we stopped at a shop for **kanji wada**. And that's where the most scientifically questionable theory of the day was born.

### 🥣 THE KANJI WADA SUPERPOWER

After eating kanji wada, the scooty started **perfectly**.

Every single time.\
 So naturally, we concluded that the shopkeeper possessed some supernatural ability.\
 Our completely serious and definitely scientifically verified theory was that he had come from the Himalayas, performed years of tapasya, and finally received the **varadan of Kanji Wada**.\
 For the next ~20 km, the scooty behaved perfectly.\
 Then the power apparently expired.💀

# CHAPTER 03 — THE ROAD TO BARABAKHARA

### *Somewhere between the highway and the hills, it started feeling like an actual trip.*

After the Kanji Wada miracle eventually wore off, we continued towards Barabakhara. We had already put in another **₹100 each for fuel**, and for a while the journey was actually going smoothly.

The road gradually changed. The familiar highway feel started to disappear, and we moved onto smaller roads leading further into the countryside. There were stretches where it was just us, the road, trees, and the landscape around us.

For once, the scooty wasn't trying to kill our plans.

### 🛵 A Brief Period of Peace

We kept going towards the **Apkhol common road** and, surprisingly, everything was working.

No stopping.\
 No pushing.\
 No random mechanical rituals.\
 No Kanji Wada required.

It almost felt like the scooty had finally accepted that we were actually going to Barabakhara. Then, after some distance, it started showing its true colours again.\
 The starting problem returned.\
 We had to stop, try again, wait, try again—and somehow keep moving. But eventually, we made it.\
 **Barabakhara.**\
 And instead of stopping where most people normally stop, we decided to go further Higher.

# CHAPTER 04 — HIGHER THAN EVERYONE ELSE

### *We could have stopped there. Obviously, we didn't.*

Most people stopped at the usual waterfall spot.

We didn't.

After reaching Barabakhara, we decided to **keep going higher**. We started hiking further up, moving away from the place where most visitors normally stayed.

The climb itself was part of the fun. There wasn't really a proper plan anymore—we were just exploring and seeing what was ahead.

### 🌳 The Tree

Somewhere along the way, we found a tree that immediately became part of the trip.

We climbed it.

### 🏞️ A Different View

As we climbed higher, the surroundings opened up. The trees, rocks and landscape started giving us views that you simply don't get from the regular stopping point.

We took more photos and kept exploring.

And then we found **another small waterfall**.

That's where the day moved from *“this is fun”* to *“okay, maybe we should be a little careful.”*

Because almost immediately after reaching it, **Abhijeet slipped.**

He somehow came out without any injury.

At the time, it was just another scary little moment.

We didn't know yet that this wouldn't even be the most dangerous thing that happened that day.

# CHAPTER 05 — WATER, ROCKS & ONE BAD DECISION

### *We came here to enjoy the waterfall. Then I decided I could swim.*

After finding the smaller waterfall, we stayed there for a while. The water was flowing over the rocks, and we were just enjoying the place. There was a section where the water came up to around **chest level**. There were two sides where we could stand, but between them was deeper water, with a few rocks in between where you could sometimes get your footing.

I don't know how to swim properly.

**I tried anyway.** 😭

### 🌊 The Middle

I started crossing the water, trying to make it to the other side in one go. For a while, everything was fine. Then, somewhere in the middle, my energy suddenly disappeared.

I tried to stand.

**My feet couldn't touch anything.**

That's when I panicked. I went underwater and started trying to figure out how to get myself out. Then something touched my leg underneath the water. I pushed against it and used whatever strength I had left to move myself forward.

Somehow, I covered the remaining distance and reached the other side.

There wasn't any dramatic rescue. I had to somehow get myself there.

Only afterward did I really understand how dangerous that moment had been. If I hadn't managed to push myself forward from underneath the water, I don't know what would have happened.

### 🫧 Somehow, We Stayed

And somehow, instead of taking that as a sign to leave, **we stayed there for almost another two hours.**

 And because apparently we are incapable of going anywhere without turning it into content, we got an idea.

# CHAPTER 06 — ONE SECOND

### *The moment the entire day changed.*

After changing clothes and finishing our time at the upper waterfall, we went back down towards the **famous spot where most people usually stop**.

<figure><img alt="IMG_0949.jpg" src="/uploads/1789324658552-img-0949.jpg"><figcaption>This is where it happened.</figcaption></figure>

We were still in the same mood as before—talking, moving around, taking photos. Nothing felt particularly different.

Then **Abhijeet slipped.**

### ⚠️ Half a Second

He is huge, and seeing him suddenly fall was honestly unbelievable.

For about **half a second, I froze**.

My brain just stopped.

Then something inside me immediately said:

**Do something.**

I had my phone in my hand. I dropped it and ran.

Priyanshu had already grabbed Abhijeet's bag. I reached him and grabbed the bag too.

I was panicking.

But deep down, I knew one thing: **Abhijeet couldn't panic.**

The rocks were extremely slippery. If he started struggling, trying to move, or suddenly tried to stand, he could lose whatever balance he still had.

So I kept telling him to stay completely still.

> **“Statue reh. Hilna mat.”**\
>  **“Main tujhe upar la raha hoon.”**\
>  **“Tu try mat kar.”**

I was scared, but I was trying to keep my voice and instructions steady enough that he wouldn't panic too.

### 🪨 Hold On

Somehow, Abhijeet managed to lock his leg against a point in the rock where there was enough friction and balance to hold him.

Priyanshu and I kept holding the bag.

For those few seconds, that was basically everything.

**Hold the bag. Don't let him move. Get him back up.**

And then, somehow, he came back.

He was safe.

### ❤️ After He Came Up

The moment he was back on stable ground, the fear didn't disappear immediately.

He hugged us.

He kept saying:

**“Sorry bro… sorry bro.”**

And then he kept thanking both of us.

I hit him lightly on the stomach and told him:

> **“Sorry sorry nahi. Bas dhyan rakhna.”**

He replied:

> **“Slip ho gaya, mein kya karta?”**

😭

That was probably the most ridiculous possible response after nearly falling off a cliff.

But he was standing.

**That's all that mattered.**

### A SMALL MEMORY BOX

> **What I remember most clearly:**\
>  I had my phone in my hand.\
>  Then I saw him fall.\
>  I dropped the phone and ran.

# CHAPTER 07 — AFTER THE FALL

### *Everyone saw what happened. Nobody really knew what to do.*

After Abhijeet came back up, people around us started shouting, scolding us and praying for us. Some told us not to make reels there and not to go near the edge. I understood them—what had just happened wasn't funny anymore. What stayed with me was that when Abhijeet actually slipped, nobody came running to help. Priyanshu had grabbed his bag first, I reached and grabbed it too, and for those few seconds we were the ones who had to get him back.

After things settled down, one person came and started asking where we were from, where we studied and what we were studying. Abhijeet somehow decided to provide completely fictional information: **Sambalpur University, Biotechnology, Cuttack.** The actual answers were VSSUT, CSE and Berhampur. 😭 A little later, some other people asked us whether that was the route. I told them yes, but warned them to be careful because a student had just fallen from the cliff and could have died if someone hadn't helped him. It felt strange saying that out loud because only minutes earlier, that student had been **our friend**.

> **A few minutes earlier we were making videos. Now we were warning strangers about the exact place where our friend had almost fallen.**

And then we left.\
 **The waterfall was behind us.**\
 **The broken scooty was waiting for us.** 💀

# CHAPTER 08 — THE SCOOTY SAGA, PART II

### *The waterfall was behind us. The real battle was apparently still on the road.*

Before leaving Barabakhara, we stopped one last time near the flowing water. We sat there for a while, and I washed my face with the cold water and took some photos. After everything we'd done at the waterfall, it felt strangely peaceful—almost like the perfect little pause before starting the journey home. We changed our clothes, packed everything up, and finally got back to the scooties.

### 🛵 And Then It Started Again

We had barely gone around **10 km** when the defective scooty stopped again. We tried starting it for around **20 minutes**. Nothing. I tried kicking it again and again, as if kicking harder would somehow convince the engine to cooperate.

![923d3bb4-4a1c-4abc-a3c8-aa3aacd43052.MP4](/uploads/1789326100621-923d3bb4-4a1c-4abc-a3c8-aa3aacd43052.mp4)

![e474ef14-99db-42ad-9cc6-47cb53a41d6c.JPG](/uploads/1789326551049-e474ef14-99db-42ad-9cc6-47cb53a41d6c.jpg)

> **Attempt #37:**\
>  Kick.\
>  Wait.\
>  Try again.\
>  Nothing.\
>  Repeat. 💀

At this point, we had already accepted that this wasn't going to be a normal journey back. So we came up with our most ridiculous solution yet. Abhijeet drove the working scooty with Priyanshu behind him, while I sat on the dead scooty and they **held my hand and pulled me along**. We went like that for roughly **10 km**. And somehow, despite having a broken vehicle in the middle of nowhere, we weren't even completely miserable—we were singing together while being towed.

### 🎵 The Soundtrack of the Breakdown

The song playing in our little roadside disaster was:

> **“Jaane Tumhe Denge Nahi.”**

Three friends. Two scooties. One engine refusing to cooperate.

And somehow we were still singing.

![9b4d5ddb-48a1-499e-8fd7-e21ac67962cb.MP4](/uploads/1789326219001-9b4d5ddb-48a1-499e-8fd7-e21ac67962cb.mp4)

> *When the engine gives up, friendship becomes horsepower.*

### 🔧 Somehow… It Started

After around 10 km of this nonsense, the scooty somehow started again.

I got back to riding and managed to take it another **30 km**. For a while, it felt like we'd finally escaped the problem.

Then it stopped again.

We tried starting it repeatedly. Nothing.

We even put some petrol into a bottle and tried that, hoping maybe fuel was somehow the problem.

Still nothing.

Eventually we stopped at a hotel and decided that if the scooty wasn't going to cooperate, at least we could eat.

![9759c960-96ca-4ae0-b7c9-acd27b5ecf44.JPG](/uploads/1789326293719-9759c960-96ca-4ae0-b7c9-acd27b5ecf44.jpg "w=58%")

**Pakodi. Jalebi.**

Honestly, at that point, that was probably more reliable than the scooty.

After eating, we tried again.

**It started.**

Five kilometres later—

**It stopped again.**

😭

So we went back to the same hand-holding towing system.

After another few kilometres, I somehow managed to start it again. By then we decided to stop trusting it completely. We switched the vehicles: **I took the good scooty with Priyanshu behind me, while Abhijeet took the problematic one.**

### 🛏️ The Khatia

During one of the stops, we came across an old-style **handmade khatia** beside the road.

After everything we'd been through, we didn't even need to discuss it.

We sat down.

Then lay down.

For a little while, we simply stopped doing anything.

It was incredibly calm. No waterfall noise, no rushing, no struggling with the engine, no trying to figure out the road. Just an old khatia and a few exhausted friends taking a break in the middle of a completely chaotic day.

We got back up, started the journey again and continued towards Burla.

### 🌧️ The Day Wasn't Finished Yet

The light was slowly disappearing.

The road ahead was getting darker.

And somewhere ahead of us, **the rain was waiting.**

We had no idea that the next few hours would become the hardest part of getting home.

# CHAPTER 09 — THE OLD ROAD HOME

### *The journey back had its own little memories.*

After leaving the waterfall area, the return journey wasn't just about getting home. In between all the scooty problems, we came across a few places that made us stop and slow down.

At one point, when the scooty stopped, we found an old-style **handmade khatia** and sat and rested there for a while. Later, we passed through a village that looked like it had somehow been left behind from another time—**mud houses, old-looking walls, and paintings of freedom fighters** on them. It looked almost like a village from the **1800s–1900s**, and I recorded a video because the whole place felt so different from the roads and towns we were used to.

### 🏡 A Village From Another Time

There was something strangely peaceful about seeing those mud houses and old walls while travelling back. We weren't looking for it; we simply happened to pass through it.

> *Sometimes the best parts of a trip are the places you never planned to visit.*

And then we continued towards home.

The day was getting late, the light was disappearing, and the weather was beginning to turn.

# CHAPTER 10 — 6:30 PM

### *When the journey stopped being fun.*

By around **6:30 PM**, it was already getting dark and the rain had become heavy. The scooty stopped again, and this time we were stuck on the road with cars and trucks passing **very close to us at high speed**. We kept trying to start it, hoping that it would somehow come back to life, but nothing worked. We asked people nearby and were told that there was a petrol pump some distance ahead, so we started pushing the scooty towards it. It took more than **45 minutes**, but finally reaching the petrol pump felt like a huge relief.

Then we filled petrol.

**Still nothing.**

We tried again and again for another 15 minutes. My phone was getting wet and repeatedly switching off, so I kept charging it with my power bank whenever I could. We desperately tried to find a mechanic, but couldn't get anyone. At one point we were so stuck that Abhijeet went one way to look for help while I tried from the other side. While he was gone, I somehow managed to start the scooty again. We came back together and decided there was only one thing left to do:

We had no idea that the rain, darkness and broken scooty were still going to give us another fight before we reached Burla.

# CHAPTER 11 — 25 KILOMETRES OF RAIN

### *At this point, we just wanted to reach home.*

After we finally managed to get the scooty started, we didn't want to risk stopping again. We drove through the **heavy rain**, already cold and completely wet, and somehow kept going. After another roughly **25 km**, the scooty stopped again. This time we tried everything and nothing worked. Around 30 minutes passed beside the road, it was dark, the headlight wasn't working, and trucks and cars were flying past us dangerously close. My whole body was already **shivering from the cold**, and my phone kept switching off because of the rain.

We had almost given up when we somehow got it working again. There was no more experimenting, no more stopping for unnecessary breaks. We just drove. **Non-stop. Through the rain.** And then finally, the NH-53 ended and we turned onto the familiar route towards Burla.

> **That turn felt like the first time all day that we were actually safe.**

### 🛣️ Finally, Burla

The moment we got back onto the Burla side after NH-53, I felt an unbelievable amount of relief. And apparently, that was the moment my body decided it was finally allowed to complain.

Suddenly I noticed a **rock inside my chappal**, my back was hurting, and there was something irritating my eye. All those tiny things I hadn't even noticed during the chaos suddenly became impossible to ignore.

*The danger was finally behind us.*\
 *My body just hadn't received the memo yet.* 😭

# CHAPTER 12 — FINALLY HOME

### *The day was finally over.*

Once we got back towards Burla, we reached **Dawat** and handed over the scooty. After everything that had happened, we finally sat down and had a proper conversation. We got some discount and ended up paying **₹500** instead of the usual ₹10–12 hour price, since we'd used it for around eight hours. The owner had actually warned us about the scooty from the beginning, and we still decided to take it anyway. 😂

And that was it.

After the waterfall, the near-drowning, Abhijeet's fall, the broken scooty, the hand-holding towing system, the khatia, the old village, darkness, heavy rain, the dead phone, the petrol pump and all the panic—we finally made it back.

> **Nobody died. Nobody got seriously injured. Somehow, the scooty survived too.**
